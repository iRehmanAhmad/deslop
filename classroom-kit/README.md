# Spot the Slop — the classroom kit

Free teaching material for one simple skill: **recognising when a website
was generated by AI on autopilot** — and understanding what a human
designer would have done differently. Built on the same ten tells the
[deslop](../README.md) scanner checks for, translated into plain words
for students.

Everything here is **free for classroom use (CC BY)** — print it, pin it,
photocopy it, share it. No accounts, no logins, no internet needed except
for the optional checker at the end.

## What's in the kit

| Piece | File | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Poster** | [`poster/poster.pdf`](poster/poster.pdf) (also `.html` source, `.png` preview) | One A4 page: the 10 tells, each with a tiny picture of the crime and one line on why it matters. For the classroom wall. |
| **Worksheet** | [`worksheet/worksheet.pdf`](worksheet/worksheet.pdf) (also `.html`, page `.png`s) | Two A4 pages. Page 1: six drawn mini-websites — five slop, one human — students circle and name the tells. Page 2: a checklist hunt on a *real* website, a Slop Score guess, and the teacher answer key for page 1 (bottom of page 2 — don't hand that part out). |
| **Quiz** | [`quiz/index.html`](quiz/index.html) | A self-contained interactive page: 10 rounds of "Slop or Not?", instant feedback naming the tell, and a final score. Works offline — double-click the file. |

## The 20-minute lesson

1. **Hook (3 min).** Ask: *"Who has seen a website that felt… fake?
   Like a robot made it in five seconds?"* Take two or three answers.
   Almost every student has felt it; today they learn to name it.
2. **Poster walk-through (5 min).** Pin up or project the poster. Read
   the 10 tells out loud — students repeat the names (they're the fun
   part). Pause on #9, the Fancy Café Look: slop changes fashion, the
   *habit* of spotting it doesn't.
3. **Worksheet hunt (8 min).** Worksheet page 1 first — five minutes,
   pencils out, circle and name. Then the reveal: **Page F is not slop.**
   Read its details out — real prices, a phone number, its own photo.
   *Specific is the opposite of slop.* If time and devices allow, finish
   page 2 on a real site; otherwise it's homework.
4. **Quiz close (4 min).** Project `quiz/index.html` and play as a class,
   voting on each round before answering. The final screen's score is
   the class's, not any one student's — keep it collective.

**For older students:** after the hunt, let them score a real website at
[deslop-checker.rehmanahmad.com](https://deslop-checker.rehmanahmad.com)
— paste a URL, get a Slop Score with every tell explained, plus a
copy-paste fix prompt for each one. Comparing the machine's score with
their own guess is the best discussion starter in the kit.

## Printing

- Poster and worksheet are A4 PDFs. Print the poster on the best colour
  settings you have; the worksheet prints acceptably in black & white
  (tells are drawn in shapes and words, not colour alone).
- Print worksheet page 2 single-sided if students will write on the
  answer-key side being visible — or simply fold/cut the key off before
  photocopying page 2 for students.
- The PNG files are previews for projecting or sharing in class chats;
  print from the PDFs for sharp text.

## Rebuilding

The PDFs and PNGs are generated from the `.html` sources:

```bash
cd classroom-kit
./build.sh   # needs WeasyPrint (pip install weasyprint) + pdftoppm (poppler-utils)
```

The quiz needs no build. Its logic and data are tested headlessly:

```bash
node classroom-kit/quiz/selftest.js
```

## A note on intent

The point is not "AI bad". The point is the difference between a page
somebody *chose* and a page that *just happened* — the unexamined
default. Students who can spot slop can also spot it in essays, images
and videos, and that's the real lesson.
