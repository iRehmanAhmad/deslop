Council votes to keep the old library open, after the reading room fills with protesters and one goat

By Margaret Ellison, Town Hall Correspondent

The council chamber had taken unusual decisions before, but rarely at this volume. By seven o'clock the public gallery held 140 residents, the entire Year Six class from St Cuthbert's, and, briefly, a goat belonging to Mr Arthur Bell of Station Road, who said it had "as much right to be heard as anyone".

The motion to close the Carnegie library passed to a second reading in September. What changed, councillors admitted on Monday, was arithmetic: the petition gathered 3,912 signatures in a town of 9,400, and the county's own figures showed the reading room issuing 61,000 loans last year, the highest per head in the county.

"We were going to save £41,000 and lose the warmest room in Millerton," said Cllr Priya Shah, who changed her vote. "My mother learned to use the internet at that library. So did half the street."

The library will now stay open on reduced hours while a trust is formed. The goat, this newspaper can confirm, has been entered in the minutes as "present, not voting".

In other news: The 7:42 to Harrogate will run on Sundays from December. The WI jam competition was won, for the fourth year, by Mrs D. Okafor. The Gazette's own printing press turned 90 and was given the afternoon off.
